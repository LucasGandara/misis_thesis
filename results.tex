\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}
In this section, we discuss the potential threats to the validity of our evaluation. While our experimental framework provides valuable insights into transfer learning for autonomous driving agents, it is important to acknowledge certain limitations that may affect the generalization and interpretation of our results. Experimental design choices, training procedures, and environmental constraints can all introduce potential biases or limitations. In this section, we discuss the primary threats to the validity of our evaluation and their potential impact on our findings.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Experiment Termination Criterion:} Our experiment termination criterion may impact result generalization. We stop an experiment when the agent successfully completes the driving course 10 consecutive times without crashes, regardless of its performance in terms of reward or efficiency metrics. This approach prioritizes safety over optimization, which may result in agents that achieve basic competence but not optimal performance.
\item \textbf{Continuous Expert Demonstrations:} We continuously provide expert demonstrations throughout the training process rather than gradually reducing them. This persistent guidance may create a dependency where agents struggle to outperform the experts they are being trained with, potentially limiting their ability to discover novel or more efficient driving strategies.

\item \textbf{Environmental maximum reward:} The maximum reward in the environment is achievable by an expert driver, and this reward is not that far from non optimal policies, this means that the agent can learn to drive in a way that is not optimal but still achieve a high reward, this is a common problem in reinforcement learning and it is known as the reward hacking problem \cite{reward_hacking}. For the Transfer ratio metric this could be a limitation.
\end{itemize}
